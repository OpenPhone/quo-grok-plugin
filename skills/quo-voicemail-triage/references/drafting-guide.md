# Drafting guide

How to write each caller's reply. The voicemail is the source; the draft answers it.

Examples below use a placeholder business. Use the actual name from the inbox, and a person's name only when the user has said who is sending.

## The shape

Four beats, under 320 characters:

1. **Who this is** — the business name.
2. **Their voicemail, specifically** — the day, and "a couple of times" if they called repeatedly. "Got your voicemail Tuesday" beats "thanks for reaching out".
3. **The substance** — what they asked about, in plain words, using their specifics. This is the part that proves a person listened.
4. **One next step** — an answer, a time, or a single question.

## By mode

The two scheduled runs catch people in different situations, and the draft should sound like it knows which.

**Morning run — they called after hours.** They expect to have reached a closed line. No apology is owed for not answering at 9pm; a prompt reply now that you're open is.
> Hi Dana, it's Ridgeline Plumbing — got your voicemail last night about the water heater. We can look at it this week. Want me to text you a couple of times that are open?

**End-of-day run — they called during business hours and got voicemail.** That's a real miss. Acknowledge it in one clause and don't explain why.
> Hi Marcus, it's Ridgeline. Sorry we missed you this afternoon. You asked about Thursday — «Thursday availability». Does a morning slot work?

**Carried forward — unanswered through at least one full run.** Name the gap with the real elapsed time, and lead with the answer rather than the apology.
> Hi Ruth, it's Ridgeline. You left a voicemail yesterday afternoon and we were slow getting back — sorry. On the permit question: we handle the filing, and it adds about a week. Want me to start it?

Never write "I just saw this" or "this slipped through". One clause, own it, move to the substance.

## Known callers

Someone with history expects you to have it. These patterns need retrieved text behind them — see the assertion rules in the skill before using any of them.

**An open thread they're following up on**
Name the thread and move it. Don't make them re-explain.
> Hi Dana, it's Ridgeline — got your voicemail about the permit. It's filed, and we're waiting on the city. I'll text you the moment it clears. Anything else you need meanwhile?

**A fact already on record**
Cite it plainly. This is the payoff of having context at all.
> Hi Tom, it's Ridgeline — got your voicemail. The quote we sent on the 3rd was for the full re-pipe. Want me to break it into stages so you can do the upstairs first?

**A figure that exists only in a call transcript**
Never state it. Transcripts garble numbers.
> Hi Ruth, it's Ridgeline — got your voicemail about the estimate. I've got the figure we discussed on the phone; let me confirm it against the file and call you back today.

**Repeat contact about the same unresolved thing**
Acknowledge the pattern factually. Don't narrate their emotions.
> Hi Sam, it's Ridgeline. This is the third time you've called about the upstairs leak and it's still not fixed — that's on us. I'd rather talk it through than text. Free at 2?

**Context contradicts the voicemail**
Don't correct them and don't concede. Get on a call.
> Hi Alex, it's Ridgeline — got your voicemail saying the work isn't finished. I want to get this straight rather than guess from my notes. Can I call you this afternoon?

**A long-standing customer, nothing special asked**
A light nod, not a performance of loyalty.
> Hi Priya, it's Ridgeline — got your voicemail. Happy to get that booked. Is the Oak Street address still right?

## By intent

**A question you can answer from retrieved history**
Answer it. Don't invite them to call back to hear something you already have in front of you.
> Hi Dana, it's Ridgeline — got your voicemail this morning about the warranty. Your install is covered on parts through next March. Want me to email the paperwork?

**A question you can't answer**
One marked slot, and the draft is **Needs your input**.
> Hi Marcus, it's Ridgeline — got your voicemail about Thursday. «Thursday availability». Does a morning slot work?

**They asked for a callback**
Honor it. The text confirms receipt; it doesn't substitute.
> Hi Ruth, it's Ridgeline. Got your voicemail — I'll call you back today before 3. If another time is easier, text me a window and I'll work around it.

**They're returning your call**
Don't apologize for missing a call they were answering. Acknowledge the crossed wires, offer a time.
> Hi Sam, it's Ridgeline — sorry we crossed wires. I'll try you again at 2, or text me a better window and I'll use that instead.

**Billing dispute or complaint**
Acknowledge the specific thing, commit to a person looking at it. No defensiveness, no policy recital.
> Hi Tom, it's Ridgeline. Got your voicemail about the duplicate charge on the September invoice. I'm pulling it up now and will call you today with what I find.

**Cancellation language**
Never argue in a text and never pitch. Get a conversation.
> Hi Priya, it's Ridgeline. Got your voicemail. I'd rather hear the full picture than guess — is there a good 10 minutes today or tomorrow?

**A new enquiry**
Repeat back what they're trying to do, then one question that moves it.
> Hi Alex, it's Ridgeline — got your voicemail about the bathroom re-pipe. That's work we do. Is the house single or two storey?

**Repeat caller, nobody got back to them**
Own the gap in one clause. No elaborate apology.
> Hi Sam, it's Ridgeline. You called three times Monday and we dropped the ball — sorry. On the leak under the sink: we can be out tomorrow morning. Does 8 to 10 work?

**No real content in the voicemail**
Short and honest. Don't invent a reason for their call.
> Hi, it's Ridgeline — got your voicemail but it came through unclear. What can I help with?

## Don't

- **Invent business facts.** Hours, prices, availability, lead times, policy. Use a slot.
- **Repeat the automated response.** They already read it.
- **Use a name you only heard in the transcript.** Speech-to-text mangles names. Unverified names go in the card, not the message.
- **Stack questions.** One.
- **Pressure.** No "limited time", no "just following up again", no "as I mentioned".
- **Apologize at length.** One clause, once, and only when something was actually dropped.
- **Quote the transcript back verbatim.** Paraphrase — transcripts are unreliable enough that quoting invites "that's not what I said".
- **Re-send a scheduling link they already ignored.** If they called instead of booking, offer a time.

## Recommended action

- **Text** — a simple question, or they asked to be texted.
- **Call, then text** — they asked for a callback, they were returning your call, or it's a complaint, billing issue, cancellation, or anything needing back-and-forth. The text confirms you got it.
- **Call now** — emergency language, a deadline inside the day, or three or more calls in an hour.
