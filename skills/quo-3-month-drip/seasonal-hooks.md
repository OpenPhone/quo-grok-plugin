# Seasonal Hooks

The seasonal angle must come from the **contact's** business cycle, not the sender's and not the calendar in the abstract. Fall means opposite things to a roofer and an accountant.

## Inferring the vertical

Check in this order and stop at the first reliable signal:

1. **Explicit statement** in a transcript or message — "we run a plumbing shop", "I'm a family law attorney".
2. **Operational vocabulary.** Trade language is distinctive: jobs, trucks, crews, dispatch, estimates, service calls (field services); matters, clients, intake, retainer (legal); patients, practice, charting, portal (healthcare); tenants, units, turnovers, work orders (property management); retainer, deliverables, scope (agencies).
3. **Pasted URLs and tooling.** Practice-management, booking, and portal links usually name the industry outright. A patient-portal link identifies a clinical practice with no other evidence needed.
4. **What they wanted Quo for.** Shared inbox for a front desk, call routing to field techs, and after-hours voicemail each imply different operations.
5. **Business name.** Weakest signal — use only as corroboration.

When the vertical is unclear, use the **generic** row. Do not guess. A wrong seasonal hook is worse than none: it proves the message was automated.

## Dormancy calibration by vertical

`dead_thread_days` should reflect the natural repurchase cycle. Suggest an override when the default 90 clearly misfits:

| Vertical | Suggested | Why |
|---|---|---|
| HVAC, plumbing, electrical | 150–180 | Semi-annual maintenance cycles; 90 days flags active customers |
| Landscaping, pool, pest | 120 | Seasonal contracts with a natural off-period |
| Moving, one-off trades | 60 | Transactional; 90 days dormant is permanently gone |
| Cleaning, salon, fitness | 60 | Recurring by nature; a lapse is a real signal fast |
| Property management | 180 | Long, event-driven cycles |
| Legal, accounting | 180+ | Engagement-based; a closed matter is not a cold lead |
| Agencies | 120 | Project-based with gaps between scopes |
| Healthcare practices | 180 | Annual or semi-annual visit cadence |

Legal and accounting need care. A closed matter is a completed engagement, not a lost opportunity — the hook should be a new-need or new-year check-in, never "we lost touch".

### Generic

Use when the vertical is unknown. Lean on universally true business rhythms rather than a trade cycle.

| Jan–Mar | Apr–Jun | Jul–Sep | Oct–Dec |
|---|---|---|---|
| New-year setup, fresh budgets | Mid-year review | Q4 planning starts | Year-end wrap, next-year planning, holiday coverage |

## Two timing cautions

**Do not text into someone's peak.** An accountant in March and a mover in July are drowning. The message will be ignored at best. When the run lands in a contact's peak column, either hold them for the shoulder period and say so, or lead by acknowledging the crunch rather than asking for their time.

**Holiday coverage is the strongest Q4 hook for small teams** across nearly every vertical — fewer people on the phone, more inbound. It works where a trade-specific hook is unavailable.

## Phrasing the hook

Reference the season as an observation about *their* business, not as a sales trigger.

- Works: "Fall is usually when the calls pick back up."
- Fails: "Fall is the perfect time to invest in your phone system."

The first sounds like someone who knows the trade. The second sounds like a campaign.
